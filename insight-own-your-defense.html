Will you own your defense, or rent it?

Vektorium Inc.22 May 2026

Originally published on LinkedIn. The publication text below is retained as published.

View original publication (opens in a new tab) ↗
Two days ago, it was reported what was inevitable. The Pentagon launched a new task force spanning U.S. Cyber Command and the NSA (National Security Agency) to determine how AI can be safely deployed across America's most sensitive classified networks. The task force was announced by Gen. Joshua Rudd, the dual-hat leader of both the NSA and Cyber Command. We have written about this exact vector trajectory three times now. 🔮 First, the most important capabilities are the ones you will not see for decades. Second, Cypher Sentinel predicted what Glasswing would reveal, and was built in anticipation of it. Third, ODNI's 2026 Annual Threat Assessment confirmed AI as a structural force reshaping ALL domains of strategic competition. Now (fourth), the Pentagon itself is racing to respond. But here is what you should not overlook in the story. The most capable AI cybersecurity model ever built, created by the company classified as a supply chain risk by the very government that needs it most. This is not a paradox. It is the logical conclusion of a model where sovereign defense depends on external providers. When the entity that holds the "keys" to your most advanced capabilities can impose conditions on how, where, and whether you use them, you do not have sovereignty. You (barely) have a license. The Pentagon signed agreements to bring AI onto classified networks. The White House is drafting an executive order requiring federal testing of frontier AI models before public release. The urgency is real. But the architecture of that urgency is what truly matters. Deploying "third-party" AI models onto sovereign networks does not make them sovereign. It makes these networks dependent on the trust, conditions, and continuity of companies whose interests may not always align with those of the state they serve. Cypher Sentinel from Vektorium was built for a different model entirely. One where the AI, the detection engines, the cryptographic layer, the verification architecture, and every line of code operate within sovereign infrastructure, under sovereign control. No external dependencies. No conditions. No licenses that can be revoked. Not because we anticipated this specific task force. But because we understood the structural reality that made it inevitable. Read again the question we posed a few weeks ago: "Will you own your defense, or rent it?" It is no longer ours alone. It is now being asked inside the Pentagon. We built the answer. Cypher Sentinel.
