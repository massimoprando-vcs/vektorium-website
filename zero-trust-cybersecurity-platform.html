Zero Trust Starts With the Resource Being Protected

NIST SP 800-207 describes a security architecture that does not grant implicit trust solely because of network location or asset ownership. Authentication and authorization are evaluated for access to a resource, with policy informed by relevant context.

For a deployment, that means answering practical questions: who is requesting access, from which device, to which resource, under what conditions, and with which privileges? Continued monitoring gives the organization a basis for revisiting access when risk changes.

Cypher Sentinel's convergence model brings those questions into the same operating environment as threat intelligence, endpoint monitoring, network analysis and security orchestration. Shared context supports a coordinated decision, while the customer's policies determine the resulting action.

Bring Identity, Device and Threat Context Together

One Operating Model Across Connected Security Functions

  • Identity and access: consider account activity, authentication context and the privileges relevant to the resource.
  • Device and endpoint: bring device posture and endpoint signals into the investigation and access evaluation.
  • Network and application: relate traffic, service activity and segmentation boundaries to the same security context.
  • Threat intelligence and analytics: connect relevant indicators and behavioral findings to the entities under investigation.
  • Policy and response: coordinate permitted actions through native capabilities and connected systems, within customer-defined limits.

Existing IAM, SIEM, EDR and network products can remain part of the environment. The integration design identifies the signals available from each selected product, the access permissions needed and the enforcement points involved in a workflow.

An Access-Risk Scenario to Explore in a Pilot

Consider a user seeking access to a sensitive application while an endpoint tool reports suspicious activity on that user's device. A useful pilot connects the identity and device evidence to the resource at risk, then demonstrates the permitted response:

  1. Receive the relevant signals. Bring the selected identity event and endpoint alert into the investigation.
  2. Build the shared context. Relate the account, device, application activity and available threat intelligence.
  3. Evaluate the agreed policy. Assess the requested access and the customer's conditions for a response.
  4. Apply the authorized workflow. Recommend an action for approval or execute a previously approved playbook through the selected enforcement point.
  5. Review the result. Examine the action, supporting evidence and operator decisions, including how exceptions are handled.

This is an illustrative evaluation scenario. The pilot establishes the actual products, signals and actions available in your environment. It gives the security team a concrete way to assess the benefit of shared context and coordinated control.

Organize the Deployment Across Five CISA Pillars

CISA's Zero Trust Maturity Model provides five pillars for organizing the work. Each becomes a practical part of the deployment discussion:

  • Identity: which identities, privileges and authentication signals are in scope?
  • Devices: how is device posture established and updated?
  • Networks: where are communication boundaries and enforcement points?
  • Applications and Workloads: which resources and service interactions need policy-driven access?
  • Data: what information is sensitive, who can use it and how is it protected?

Use the selected framework to define scope and evidence, then test the relationships between these areas. Frequently Asked Questions

What is a zero trust cybersecurity platform?

A zero trust cybersecurity platform brings identity, device, resource and policy context together to support access decisions and ongoing monitoring. Cypher Sentinel combines those functions with native security capabilities and selected external integrations under customer-defined authority.

Does zero trust require replacing our existing IAM, SIEM or endpoint tools?

No. Cypher Sentinel is designed to work alongside existing tools through selected bridges and APIs. The deployment identifies which systems provide identity, device and threat signals, where policy is enforced and which response actions are permitted.

What are the five pillars of the CISA Zero Trust Maturity Model?

CISA’s Zero Trust Maturity Model groups the work into Identity, Devices, Networks, Applications and Workloads, and Data. These five pillars provide a way to organize a deployment assessment; the model also includes three cross-cutting capabilities.

Who decides whether Cypher Sentinel can act automatically?

The customer sets the authority for each workflow. Cypher Sentinel can be scoped to recommendations, execution of approved playbooks or autonomous actions within defined policy. Higher-impact actions escalate for approval according to the agreed operating model.

How does zero trust reduce the impact of compromised credentials?

Least-privilege access, device assessment, resource-level policy and continued monitoring can limit what a compromised account can reach. The deployment must define the relevant enforcement points and response workflows, then demonstrate how they behave when identity or device risk changes.

What should a zero trust pilot demonstrate?

A pilot should show a defined access or investigation workflow from initial signals through policy evaluation and authorized action. Review the evidence used, the permissions applied, the operator’s control, the enforcement result and how exceptions or loss of a signal are handled.
