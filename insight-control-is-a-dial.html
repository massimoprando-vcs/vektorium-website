Control is a dial, not a flag.

Vektorium Inc.16 October 2026

Originally published on LinkedIn. The publication text below is retained as published.

View original publication (opens in a new tab) ↗
Last week, Stanford. Its motto: "the wind of freedom blows." For two days at the Hoover Institution, it did. Stanford DEFCON put a former President of Taiwan, the Chairman of the Joint Chiefs, and the Director of the Defense Innovation Unit on the same stage as the people building autonomous fleets, drones in weeks, contested logistics, new cyber, nuclear power, and commercial intelligence. Eighteen sessions, all saying the same thing. Fragmented strategies are losing to integrated ecosystems of systems. Freedom and democracy are defended by whoever controls the technology that defends them. And while we were in the room, Washington moved. On September 30, the Pentagon created Project Agincourt. Its destination: a four-star Autonomous Warfare Command by October 1, 2027. Days later, US Tech Force opened its next cohort: engineers brought inside government for two-year tours. Owen West, who closed Stanford DEFCON, is the CEO of Project Agincourt. Scott Kupor, who spoke on the call to service, runs US Tech Force. The people on stage are the people building the structure. One word was in every room: sovereign. One problem: it has become a label. Sovereignty, as engineering, is not a data center inside your border. It is a set of questions: A building inside your country says where the machines sit. Not who can read your data, stop your service, or change how your model behaves. Three things an outsider can do to you. Read. Stop. Change. Then look at where control sits: Who runs the machines, who holds the keys, which law can give the order, who owns the model, who makes the chips, who supplies the power. Decide which one would hurt. Buy control over that one. Not the label on the brochure. Rent the best models and cyber software. But keep one you can run yourself, strong enough to keep critical work alive. Move a real workload to it and see what breaks. A fallback you have never used is a guess. Control is a dial, not a flag. Two months ago, we wrote here: No switch in someone else's hands. At Stanford, we sharpened it. Sovereignty is not the absence of someone else's switch. It is knowing where that switch is, what happens if it is used, and whether you have a workable alternative. Cypher Sentinel was engineered for the highest setting of that dial. • Keys and ciphertext in your custody. • Post-quantum, homomorphic, zero-knowledge. • Zero external dependencies. It runs with the outside world switched off. • Every engine verified from inside the boundary. Nothing alters behavior without your approval. Sovereign is not a region we sell. It is a mindset we engineered. Governments and enterprises now arrive at the question we have asked since March: Will you own your defense, or rent it? Name the verb. Buy the rung. Rehearse the exit.
