Not every outage has an attacker. Every outage has an owner.

Vektorium Inc.18 November 2026

Originally published on LinkedIn. The publication text below is retained as published.

View original publication (opens in a new tab) ↗
Since Monday, hundreds of people, if not thousands, have asked us to comment on what is happening. So, here it is... Email going dark across the world for about 18 hours. Every departure in the United States grounded (third time in history). Banks. Apple’s services. Multiple networks. The NSA’s own classified network. All inside one week. The official answer, every time: it was not a cyberattack. This was meant to reassure. The provocative thing: a cyberattack would have been by far the better answer. An attack has a culprit. Someone to name, to sanction, to deter. What is happening is far worse. A router swap. A configuration change. An internal update. A routine patch. An enemy was not even needed. Hence, here it is: Not every outage has an attacker. Every outage has an owner. Asked why the FAA could not simply change supplier, the US Transportation Secretary said of its contractor: “I can’t go somewhere else. They own it.” Key word: They. And where an enemy did show up, the door was already open: Before that, the fridges and PHP. On Monday 178 organisations, CNN, the BBC, Zoom and HSBC among them, served malware to their own visitors for about 17 hours. None of them was breached. A vendor’s script changed overnight. And Google documented the first intrusion run end to end by AI, with no human in the loop. Username admin and today’s date as the password, on controllers unsupported for years. Different causes. One structure. The failure sat in something the victim did not own, could not see, and could not stop. In our previous post, we wrote that an outsider can do three things to you: Read. Stop. Change. This month, all three happened at scale. It will accelerate. A year ago Microsoft shipped about 100 security fixes a month. Now about 1,100. Machines find the flaws, machines turn patches into exploits, and systems were never built to be changed at that pace. Cypher Sentinel was engineered for exactly this. Zero external dependencies. It runs with the outside world switched off. Every engine verified from inside the boundary. Nothing alters behavior without your approval. The question is no longer only who attacks you. It is who owns what you stand on. We asked in every post: "Will you own your defense, or rent it?"
