Protect the Service, the Process and the Data

Operational technology includes industrial control systems (ICS) and supervisory control and data acquisition (SCADA) systems used to monitor and control physical processes.

A cybersecurity decision in an operational environment can affect a physical process. Critical infrastructure protection therefore has to account for service continuity, engineering constraints and the people responsible for safe operation.

NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3 addresses the distinct performance, reliability and safety requirements of operational technology. These requirements belong in the security workflow from the start: how data is collected, how an alert is assessed and who can authorize a change to an operational system.

A Workflow to Evaluate: Remote Engineering Access

Consider an illustrative pilot scenario involving suspicious use of a remote engineering account. Bring the approved identity, endpoint and monitoring signals into the investigation. Review the asset's criticality, the work being performed and the maintenance window. The responsible operator then chooses the permitted action and records the decision. This is a workflow to validate against your systems and procedures during the pilot.

Shape the Scope Around the Essential Service

The same convergence architecture can be evaluated against different operating requirements. Define the important assets and service dependencies before selecting a technology scope.

  • Energy and utilities: prioritize continuity, engineering access and the boundary between enterprise systems and control environments.
  • Water and industrial operations: identify the processes, equipment constraints and authorized maintenance activity that give an alert operational meaning.
  • Communications and transportation: map service dependencies, distributed administration and the effect of a response on connected operations.
  • Financial infrastructure: establish the identity, transaction-service and evidence requirements for the systems included in the pilot.

Each pilot should document the devices, protocols, versions and existing monitoring tools it depends on. Confirm the required coverage and collection method for that environment before extending the scope.

Automation must fit the operating authority. Distinguish observation and recommendations from approval-gated actions and any bounded automated workflows. For actions that could affect production or safety, define the responsible decision maker, operational checks and recovery procedure before enabling the workflow.

Build a Pilot Around Operational Safety

A useful pilot is narrow enough to assess and meaningful enough to inform the next deployment decision. Start with one service or site and a threat scenario the security and operations teams agree matters.

  • Establish the boundary: identify the assets, operating constraints, permitted data sources and people accountable for the pilot.
  • Verify collection: agree the collection method, including passive or existing telemetry where appropriate, and assess its operational impact.
  • Exercise the workflow: investigate the chosen scenario with the selected integrations, agreed approvals and recovery procedure.
  • Review the evidence: compare data completeness, investigation effort, time to an approved decision and operational impact with the documented baseline.

Move from the pilot to broader deployment through an acceptance decision for the systems and capabilities evaluated. Use the results to refine integration coverage, operational ownership and the next use case.

Make Assurance Evidence Part of the Architecture

Use your applicable sector requirements and internal operating standards to define the controls and evidence needed. CISA's Cross-Sector Cybersecurity Performance Goals provide a voluntary, prioritized baseline of security practices. NIST's OT security guidance provides a reference for the constraints of industrial and other physical systems.

For each requirement, identify the relevant platform function or existing tool, the configuration, the responsible owner and the evidence to review. A documented implementation and assessment establish the assurance case for the actual environment.

Prepare Long-Lived Infrastructure for Cryptographic Change

Include cryptography in the asset and dependency review: where keys and certificates are used, which communications need protection, how software updates are authenticated and what equipment can be changed. Frequently Asked Questions

What is critical infrastructure cybersecurity?

Critical infrastructure cybersecurity protects the systems that support essential services, including energy, water, communications, transport and finance. It connects enterprise security with operational requirements so that decisions account for service continuity, physical processes, asset criticality and the authority of the responsible operator.

How is OT cybersecurity different from IT cybersecurity?

Operational technology monitors or controls physical processes. Its security plan must account for safety, reliability, timing and equipment constraints as well as confidentiality and data integrity. NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3 addresses those requirements; a response suitable for an office endpoint may be inappropriate for a production controller.

Can Cypher Sentinel work alongside existing OT security tools?

The integration-first approach connects selected existing tools and approved data sources to the convergence platform. The pilot defines the required interfaces, data direction, product versions and permissions, then verifies the relevant telemetry and workflows in the operator environment.

Which industrial protocols and devices should be checked before deployment?

Start with an inventory of the protocols, controllers, gateways, device versions and monitoring tools actually in use. Agree the required data collection method and supported interfaces for each pilot workflow. Validate the selected coverage and operational impact before extending the deployment to additional systems.

Can automated response interrupt a critical process?

Actions that affect production or safety must remain within operator-defined authority. The deployment plan should distinguish observation, recommendations, approval-gated actions and any bounded automation. For OT environments, response design must include operational impact, maintenance windows, recovery procedures and the responsible decision maker.

What is the best starting point for a critical infrastructure pilot?

Choose one service or site, one material threat scenario and the existing tools needed to observe it. Define the data boundary, operational constraints, permitted actions and acceptance measures. Validate the integration and investigation workflow before considering wider coverage or more automated response.
