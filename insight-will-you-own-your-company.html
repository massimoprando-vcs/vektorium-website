Will you own your defense? Will you own your company?

Vektorium Inc.26 June 2026

Originally published on LinkedIn. The publication text below is retained as published.

View original publication (opens in a new tab) ↗
Yesterday, Ashwin Gopinath observed what happens when an AI vendor becomes your team member. When its agent sits inside your Slack, follows your threads, remembers your context, routes your work, and holds your institutional memory. His conclusion: "You are now renting your company back from them." His post went viral. Well over 1 million views. At Vektorium Inc., we have been writing about it for months. We recognized this pattern 3 years ago in cybersecurity. Here is where the consequences of dependency arrive fastest. The arc we described with our previous 5 posts is clear. - The most important capabilities are invisible. - Will you own your defense, or rent it? - The structural force reshaping the domains of competition. - The Pentagon responds: Sovereignty vs. Dependency. - Can you verify what you own? Verification is the precondition for sovereignty. The question was always bigger than defense. Defense is just where the consequences land first. Who controls the security layer? Who holds the context layer? The workflows, the memory, the institutional knowledge of your company, the operational logic that makes your organization what it is? Models can be swapped, and agents can be replicated. Once your operating memory lives inside a vendor's architecture, you are not a customer. You are a tenant. And tenants do not set the terms. We built Cypher Sentinel to solve this. Nothing - not the intelligence, not the detection, not the memory, not the verification - leaves the sovereign boundary. Endogenous. Sovereign. Entropy. It evolves, adapts, and generates entropy from within. No external dependencies. No context surrendered. No keys held by someone else. We did not build Cypher Sentinel because we anticipated this specific conversation. We built it because we understood the structural reality that made it inevitable. "Will you own your defense?
